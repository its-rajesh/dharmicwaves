Downfall of Dravidian Ideology I

E. V. Ramasamy: The Man Who Tried to Break India from Within

Published Opinion Essay | For historical and educational analysis only

Introduction

E. V. Ramasamy is often presented in Tamil Nadu as a revolutionary reformer. His supporters call him a social justice icon. His statues stand across Tamil Nadu, and political parties still claim his legacy today. However, beneath the propaganda lies a hard truth: he used hate-filled separatist politics that divided Tamil society, attacked Indian unity, rejected the national identity, and encouraged anti-Hindu hostility according to numerous critics and historians[1][2]. This article exposes the separatist ideology he spread and how it damaged the national integrity of India.

The Real Agenda Behind His Politics

Although he claimed to fight for “rationalism” and “self-respect,” his political campaign was built on extreme separatism. He openly rejected the idea of one India and demanded the breakup of the country by forming a separate nation called Dravida Nadu[3][4]. In multiple speeches, he declared that Tamils must not accept the Indian national identity.

“தமிழர்களுக்கு இந்தியாவில் நீதி கிடைக்காது. திராவிட நாடு மட்டுமே வாழ்வு தரும்.”
“Tamils will not get justice in India. Only a Dravidian nation will save us.”
— Attributed to E. V. Ramasamy in Dravidian movement periodicals[4]

This wasn’t accidental rhetoric. It was a systematic political campaign against India.

How He Spread Division and Hate

E. V. Ramasamy used hate speech, propaganda, and social engineering to mobilize anger rather than reform. Instead of positive change, he promoted community hatred. He deliberately created hostility among Tamils toward North Indians, Brahmins, Hindi speakers, and Hindu believers.

He repeatedly used verbal abuse in speeches to seduce crowds emotionally:

“கடவுள் இல்லை, கடவுளை உருவாக்கியவர் முட்டாள்!”
“There is no god; the one who created god is a fool!”[17]

“இந்துயிசம் என்பது விஷம்.”
“Hinduism is poison.”[12]

These inflammatory statements did not reform society — they destroyed civil debate and encouraged hate-driven politics.

His Opposition to Mahatma Gandhi

E. V. Ramasamy hated Gandhi with extreme intensity. In fact, he celebrated Gandhi’s assassination by blaming Gandhi for trying to unite India through non-violence[5]. He wrote that Gandhi protected the caste system, a claim refuted by historians who highlight Gandhi’s anti-untouchability campaigns[18].

He openly wrote and spoke:

“காந்தி தமிழர்களின் பகைவர்.”
“Gandhi is the enemy of Tamils.”[5]

His criticism was not intellectual — it was personal and abusive.

His Lies About B. R. Ambedkar

Modern Dravidian propagandists falsely link E. V. Ramasamy with Dr. B. R. Ambedkar, but the truth is:

  • Ambedkar never worked with him politically
  • Ambedkar never supported Dravida Nadu
  • Ambedkar supported national unity, not secession

Ambedkar fought caste with constitutional reform, while E. V. Ramasamy burned the Constitution in protest[8]. Their ideologies did not align.

His Hatred Toward K. Kamaraj

K. Kamaraj, called the Kingmaker of India, was a Tamil leader who built schools, industries, dams, and roads across Tamil Nadu[16]. But E. V. Ramasamy hated him because Kamaraj united Tamils with India rather than dividing them. He attacked Kamaraj using abusive language during public speeches.

His War Against Indian Identity

E. V. Ramasamy never accepted Indian independence in 1947. Instead, he claimed:

“இந்திய சுதந்திரம் போலி.”
“Indian independence is fake.”[14]

He refused to fly the Indian flag, rejected the national anthem, and encouraged his followers to disrespect independence day celebrations[9].

The Anti-Hindi Hate Campaign

The anti-Hindi protests he led in Tamil Nadu in 1938 and 1965 were not about language rights—they were political weapons used to create hate against North Indians[10]. Instead of promoting Tamil language proudly, he chose linguistic violence.

He repeatedly said:

“இந்தி என்பது அடிமைத்தனம்.”
“Hindi is a form of slavery.”[10]

Attacks on Hindu Culture

He openly attacked Hindu festivals, temples, and scriptures. He burned copies of the Ramayana and held obscene public demonstrations to insult Hindu beliefs[12]. His writings like “Suyamariyathai” and “Ramayana: A True Review” insulted Hindu deities.

But did he ever attack any other religion like this? No. Because his target was India’s civilizational foundation — Hindu Dharma.

Psychological Damage to Tamil Nadu

His politics created an inferiority complex in Tamil minds by endlessly repeating lies:

  • That Tamil Nadu was enslaved by North India (false)
  • That Hindu Dharma is evil (false)
  • That Brahmins are enemies (hate speech)
  • That India stole Tamil identity (false)
  • That separatism is freedom (dangerous lie)

This poisoned Tamil political culture for decades. Even today, some political parties use his anti-India narrative to maintain vote banks through division.

Evidence of His Separatist Agenda

He demanded:

  1. Dravida Nadu secession[3]
  2. Tamil Nadu’s exit from India[4]
  3. Rejection of Indian Constitution[8]
  4. Replacement of national institutions with “Dravidian” identity[6]

These are facts on record, not opinions. His speeches are documented in Dravidian party publications, archives, and books.

Why Calling Him a Social Reformer Is a Myth

A true social reformer unites people, uplifts society, fights injustice with wisdom, and inspires progress. But E. V. Ramasamy:

✘ Spread hate, not reform
✘ Destroyed spirituality instead of promoting reason
✘ Pushed for division instead of unity
✘ Performed vulgar drama in the name of protest
✘ Failed to build schools, hospitals, literature, or institutions
✘ Never improved Tamil Nadu economically or educationally
✘ Left behind only anger and division

The Legacy of Division

Tamil Nadu produced giants of wisdom and patriotism:

  • Subramania Bharati
  • V. O. Chidambaram Pillai
  • K. Kamaraj
  • Sir P. S. Sivaswami Iyer
  • Dr. Abdul Kalam

These leaders loved Tamil culture but also loved India. Unlike them, E. V. Ramasamy preached bitterness and separation.

Conclusion — Time to Remove the Toxic Mindset

The truth is clear: E. V. Ramasamy was not a freedom fighter, not a nation builder, and not a positive reformer. He was a divisive political agitator whose ideology still threatens India’s unity. His words did not build Tamil Nadu — they burned bridges of peace and brotherhood.

It is time for Tamil Nadu to rise above outdated hatred, separate propaganda from history, and embrace national unity with cultural pride. India is not Tamil Nadu’s enemy — India is Tamil Nadu’s home.
